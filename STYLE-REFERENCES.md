# Style References

Visual DNA uses real creative references to understand recurring visual characteristics across fashion, photography, film, magazines, architecture and advertising.

The references are **lineage and research cues**, not instructions to reproduce a specific campaign, copyrighted image, logo, set or artist's exact work.

## Reference map

| Visual School | Reference areas | What we extract |
|---|---|---|
| Editorial Minimalism | contemporary fashion retail/editorial, luxury studio photography | restraint, silhouette, negative space, controlled light |
| Surreal Minimalism | Jacquemus-type contemporary fashion campaigns, surreal landscape editorials | scale, open space, graphic staging, one unexpected idea |
| Raw Documentary | documentary fashion photography, raw editorial traditions | real locations, imperfection, candid behavior, available light |
| 90s Cinema | 35mm crime drama, neo-noir and character cinema | practical light, halation, grain, narrative framing |
| Flash Tabloid | paparazzi and wire-service photography, fashion campaigns using paparazzi language | direct flash, intrusive framing, motion, imperfect crops |
| Dark Luxury | contemporary luxury fashion, beauty and watch advertising | darkness, material richness, sculptural highlights |
| Maximalist Narrative | Italian fashion advertising and maximalist editorial | layered set design, color, theatrical narrative |
| Cinematic Travel | luxury travel campaigns and cinematic fashion films | environmental scale, movement, destination as narrative |
| Magazine Portrait | Esquire, GQ, Monocle and contemporary editorial portraits | mature confidence, meaningful environments, controlled crops |
| New Hollywood Intimacy | character-driven American cinema and intimate film portraiture | lived-in spaces, close observation, practical light |
| Urban 2000s Grit | early-2000s street, music and fashion photography | direct flash, city materials, early-digital texture |
| Scandinavian Quiet | Scandinavian fashion, interiors and lifestyle editorial | daylight, functionality, muted materials, calm composition |
| Analog Family Album | consumer 35mm snapshots and disposable-camera culture | proximity, grain, imperfect focus, everyday emotion |
| Hyperreal Sports Campaign | premium sports advertising and action photography | physical realism, speed, impact, dramatic perspective |
| Neo-Futurist | technology campaigns, contemporary architecture and speculative editorial | reflective materials, geometry, cool light, controlled futurism |
| Dreamscape | poetic editorial photography and dreamlike cinema | haze, reflection, ambiguity, soft surreal atmosphere |
| Pop Graphic Studio | pop advertising, graphic design, contemporary studio campaigns | color systems, hard shadows, geometric staging |
| Brutalist Editorial | brutalist architecture photography and architectural fashion editorials | concrete, scale, severe geometry, long shadows |
| Quiet Humanism | humanist documentary photography and observational cinema | authentic behavior, subtle gesture, emotional credibility |
| Experimental Optical | experimental fashion photography and optical experimentation | reflections, glass, distortion, layered framing |

## Important principle

A reference should explain **why a visual direction looks the way it does**. Do not turn the reference into a command to imitate a living artist or recreate a specific campaign.

Translate references into observable characteristics: composition, light, color, material, environment, behavior, camera distance, optical phenomena and narrative structure.
